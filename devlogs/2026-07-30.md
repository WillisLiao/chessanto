### Principal findings

Full detail lives in `handoffs/NEXT-SESSION-ANALYSIS-CORRECTNESS.md`.
The summary judgement is that the architecture is strong and the numbers feeding it are not.

**The single highest-leverage defect is analysis depth.**
`EngineService.swift:21-27` uses movetime rather than depth, with standard at 350 milliseconds per position.
The classification thresholds distinguish a 9-point win-probability drop from an 11-point one, which is below the evaluation noise floor at that budget in sharp positions.
Movetime is also not reproducible, so re-analysis produces different classifications, different key moments, and a churning spaced-repetition queue.
Every teaching surface in the app is downstream of these numbers.

**Four correctness holes compound it.**
No book move exemption despite `deepestBookPly` already being computed by `ReportBuilder.buildOpeningFact`.
No forced-move handling, and `WinProbability.fromMate` returning a hard 0 or 100 means every move after an opponent's forced mate appears as a blunder.
`Accuracy.average` is a plain arithmetic mean where Lichess uses a volatility-weighted and harmonic combination, so the percentage is not comparable to the one users will compare it against.
`ThemeDetector.punishment` fires on any capture-first principal variation, so ordinary trades render as hanging pieces, and that false fact propagates into practice card themes, Player Brief motifs, and the takeaway generator.

**`MoveClassification.brilliant` is assigned nowhere** despite having a color, a `!!` mark, a glossary entry, and a coach headline.

**`KeyMomentSelector` does not filter to the user's side**, so the eight-slot register mixes both players while `TrainingCardFactory` filters afterward, which is why the register and the practice card count disagree.

**The flow locks the valuable surfaces behind a chore.**
Player Brief, the accuracy trend, motifs, and the review queue all need many analyzed games, and analysis is manual, one game at a time, with no batch action and no analyze-on-import.
Onboarding collects a chess.com username and then lands the user on an empty window with an "Import PGN…" button.
Player Brief is hard-gated behind chess.com confirmation, so a PGN-only user, explicitly supported by the README, can never see their progress.

**The board will read as broken to a beginner.**
`BoardView` has no drag and drop, no piece animation, no sounds, and no right-click annotation arrows.
`GameReplayViewModel.playMove` auto-queens with no picker, and `PracticeSessionViewModel.select` builds a four-character UCI that cannot match a five-character promotion `bestMoveUCI`, so a promotion practice card is probably ungradeable.
That last one needs direct reproduction before any fix, per the repository's bug-fix rule.

**The teaching level setting is cosmetic.**
`RatingRegister` injects one sentence into the LLM system prompt and changes nothing else.
Beginners and advanced players receive identical thresholds, moment counts, principal variation lengths, practice cards, and grading strictness.

**The LLM Coach is currently a liability.**
The committed screenshot shows it answering an open-ended question with three tautologies, repeating the identical answer verbatim, and promising an evaluation check it never performs.
The verifier passed it because the moves were legal and no evaluation was claimed, which exposes the structural limit: grounding proves nothing false was said, not that anything useful was said.
`CoachModelCatalog` defaults to `qwen3:4b` under 16GB, so this is the default experience.

### Recommended order

1. Analysis correctness: fixed depth, book exemption, forced and decided positions, accuracy aggregation, punishment gating.
2. Flow: batch analysis, onboarding that ends in an analyzed game, land on Review, unlock Player Brief for PGN-only users.
3. Board: drag and drop, promotion picker and the promotion practice bug, animation, sounds, annotation arrows.
4. Teaching: make the rating register do real work, add the detectors that explain real games, fix the takeaway generator, implement `brilliant`, multi-ply practice cards, a real spaced-repetition scheduler, beginner vocabulary.
5. Decide what the Coach is for.

## Priority 1 implementation: analysis correctness

### Corrections to the review document

**Already-lost positions do not flood the report with blunders.**
The review claimed `WinProbability.fromMate` returning a hard 0 or 100 meant every move after an opponent's forced mate would register a 100-point drop.
It does not.
Once the mover's win probability is pinned at 0 the drop is `max(0, 0 - 0)`, so those moves grade `.excellent`.
Probed directly against `MoveClassifier` before writing any code, and the behaviour is now pinned by `movesInAnAlreadyLostPositionDoNotGradeAsBlunders`.
The real defect in that area was narrower: forced moves were graded `.best`, inflating both accuracy and the best-move count.

**The depth-12-reports-11 symptom is not a delivery race.**
Eight depth-12 searches reported depth 11 in five of them, which looks exactly like chesskit-engine's unordered response dispatch losing the final `info` behind the terminating `bestmove` - and the first fix assumed that.
`engine-smoke` now drains everything still in flight by running a second search and consuming to its own bestmove.
The depth never improves, so the line is never emitted at all and no consumer-side wait can recover it.
The ±1 ply is inherent to Stockfish under MultiPV with multiple threads.
`BoundedSearchSession`'s grace window, `settle()`, and `BatchCollector.hasReachedDepth` were removed along with the six tests covering them.

**The first depth presets were unreachable.**
Depth 18 with a 4-second ceiling reached 18 on only 14% of a 50-position game; the rest were cut off between 14 and 17, with the ceiling binding as the norm rather than as a backstop.
Retuned to depth 12/16/20 with 5s/20s/60s ceilings, then re-measured: 41 of 50 positions at depth 16 and 9 at 15.

### What changed

- **Depth budgeting.** `AnalysisQuality` carries a depth and a movetime ceiling instead of a movetime. Do not combine both limits in one UCI `go` - Stockfish then reports one iteration fewer - so the ceiling is enforced with an explicit `stop`, which also turns a ceiling hit into usable partial lines rather than a thrown error.
- **Analysis reuse.** Decided by the preset label alone, so rows written when "standard" meant 350ms would be kept under a preset now promising a depth. The real library had a game stored as "standard" whose rank-one depths ran 8 to 17. Reuse now requires the stored depth to meet the requested depth, and the UI reports the shallowest depth actually stored.
- **`.book` and `.forced` classifications.** `deepestBookPly` was already computed and discarded, so mainline theory was being flagged as inaccuracy. Both are counted and shown but excluded from accuracy. `ClassificationContext.forGame` is the single source of truth so the phone companion cannot disagree with the Mac.
- **Punishment facts.** Fired on any capture-first PV, so ordinary trades rendered as hanging pieces and fed the practice themes, the Player Brief motifs, and the takeaway rule. Now decided by replaying the capture and asking whether the piece can be taken back, with an even-length settle for genuine exchanges and a checkmate bypass.
- **Key moments.** Filtered to the user's own side when known, so the register and the practice card count stop disagreeing.
- **Accuracy.** Replaced the arithmetic mean with Lichess's volatility-weighted plus harmonic combination, fetched from `lichess-org/lila` rather than reconstructed. Fixture game: White 94 to 90, Black 91 to 75.
- **Training grading.** Compared a 500ms search of the attempted move against the card's stored deep search; now searches one ply shallower than the card's own reference depth.
